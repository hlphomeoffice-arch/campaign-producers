Brand and performance are one campaign, not two budgets.

Performance captures demand that exists. Brand helps create and shape the demand that comes next. Split them and both have to work harder.

By Henk PretoriusFounder / Campaign Producer

When money gets tight, performance usually wins the argument. It has dashboards, leads and a cost per acquisition. Brand arrives with a story about future value. The technology founder looks at both and makes the reasonable decision to fund the thing that appears closest to revenue.

That decision can become expensive. Performance marketing is good at capturing people who are ready to act. It cannot, by itself, make enough people know you, understand you or prefer you before that moment arrives. When every advert asks for the sale, the campaign has skipped the reason to buy.

Do not run a memorable campaign over here and a measurable campaign over there. Build one idea that earns attention, creates memory and gives people a clear next step.

The split is an operating problem

WARC's 2026 Multiplier Playbook examines why organisations struggle to join brand building and performance advertising. Its supporting study with the Association of National Advertisers polled 209 marketers from ANA member organisations. WARC later reported that about two-thirds had siloed brand and performance budgets, roughly half operated separate teams and only 8 per cent strongly agreed that their teams knew how to deliver integrated advertising in practice.

This matters because the customer never sees the organisation chart. They see a founder video, a product page, a paid social advert, a search result and a sales call. If each touchpoint carries a different idea, the customer has to do the integration work. Most will not.

Performance captures today. Brand improves tomorrow.

The Ehrenberg-Bass Institute's B2B research argues that only a small proportion of potential buyers are in market at any one time. Its widely used 95:5 rule is a rule of thumb, not a universal ratio for every category. The useful principle is simpler: most of the people who can buy from you later are not shopping today.

Performance activity can help the active buyer find, compare and act. Brand activity helps the future buyer remember who you are and what problem you solve. A technology company needs both jobs done. Otherwise it keeps paying to interrupt the same small pool of active demand while remaining unknown to the larger group that will enter the market later.

One customer promise should organise the whole campaign

Integration does not mean making every asset identical. It means every asset is recognisably helping the same customer make the same important change. Start with a promise the product can prove and the customer cares about. Then decide how that promise must work at different moments.

  • For people who do not know you, dramatise the problem or desired change in a way they will remember.
  • For people exploring the category, show useful expertise and a clear point of view.
  • For people comparing options, provide demonstrations, evidence and customer proof.
  • For people ready to act, remove friction and make the next step obvious.

The expression changes. The strategic spine does not. A founder story, a signature social format, a hero film, a case study and a paid advert can all do different jobs while reinforcing the same promise.

Build a creative platform, not an asset pile

Cheaper production has made it easier to generate more content. It has not made attention cheaper. CreativeX analysed 633,000 advertisements over two years, and WARC reported in July 2026 that content production had risen by 29 per cent while media spend per advert had fallen by 15 per cent. The study covers large advertisers and should not be treated as a forecast for every business, but it identifies a useful risk: more assets can leave less support behind each one.

The answer is not one expensive film and silence. It is a repeatable creative platform. Find a format and point of view that your ideal customer values, make it recognisably yours, test how it performs and put meaningful distribution behind the strongest expression. Repetition should build memory, not boredom. Keep the promise and distinctive cues consistent while changing the story, proof and invitation.

Do not use one number to judge two different jobs

A short-term response advert and a story designed to build future demand should not be judged on the same day or by the same metric. That does not excuse vague measurement. It requires a joined plan with different indicators connected to the same commercial outcome.

  • Memory and meaning: Are more of the right people recognising the company, recalling the promise and associating it with a relevant buying situation?
  • Demand behaviour: Are direct visits, branded searches, qualified engagement and return visits moving?
  • Commercial response: Are opportunities, conversion quality, sales velocity, acquisition cost and revenue changing?

WARC's 2025 Multiplier Effect brought together evidence from Analytic Partners, BERA.ai, Prophet, System1 and WARC. It reported that moving from a performance-focused approach to a more balanced advertising strategy could lift overall revenue returns by between 25 and 100 per cent in the analysed evidence. That range is a benchmark, not a promise for an individual campaign. The practical lesson is that short-term efficiency can be misleading when it ignores the demand and brand equity that made the response possible.

Give one person responsibility for the multiplication

Integration usually fails in the handovers. Strategy defines the audience. Brand writes the story. Content fills the calendar. Performance rewrites the message for conversion. Sales creates another deck. Each team can do its part well while the campaign becomes weaker.

One accountable campaign owner should protect the customer promise across strategy, creative, production, distribution and measurement. Specialists still bring their judgement. The owner makes sure each decision strengthens the next one. The goal is not control for its own sake. It is cumulative value.

What this means for your next campaign

Put the brand and performance teams, budgets and briefs on one page. Define the commercial change, the audience, the customer promise and the proof. Decide what must be remembered before deciding what must be clicked. Build one creative platform that can carry stories, evidence and response. Give the best work enough distribution to learn properly. Then measure memory, demand and revenue on timelines appropriate to each job.

A campaign should not choose between making people care and making it easy to buy. It should do both, in the right sequence, under one idea.
